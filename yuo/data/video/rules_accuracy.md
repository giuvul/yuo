Accuracy rules (from the factual errors an expert reviewer found in earlier videos):
- Simplify by leaving details out, never by replacing the real mechanism with a wrong one. Name who does
  what correctly at a newcomer's level (e.g. "plasma cells, which B cells turn into, release antibodies";
  "T cells recognise fragments shown to them by other cells", not "T cells grab the virus").
- No absolute claims unless they are strictly true: avoid "always", "never", "instantly", "guarantees",
  "all", "you never get sick". Say "usually", "typically", "within days", "greatly reduces the risk".
- When a common real-world variant exists, say "typically" or mention it in a few words (e.g. the server's
  ACK and FIN are often combined into one segment).
- Numbers must be realistic (timescales, sizes, percentages). If a chart only illustrates a shape, label
  it "illustrative" on screen and keep the timescale realistic.
- Use the standard textbook terms and definitions; do not invent names for things.
- Before you reply, reread every sentence and every on-screen label and ask: would a university lecturer
  in this subject mark it wrong or misleading? If so, fix it.
